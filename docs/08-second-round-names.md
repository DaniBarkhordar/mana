## Dead this round, and why it matters

Twenty-nine of forty-four. The ones worth noting because they show the pattern again:

- **Fettle** — SH:24's live UK at-home testing brand, Class 44. That is your stage-3 product, already on the name.
- **Kilter** — Kilter Inc., US Class 9 "apps for tracking health activity", registered 2019.
- **Calder** — CarePredict's live US Class 9 mark for "wearable activity trackers… fitness, health and wellness data".
- **Marrow** — Marrow Health, Class 5 vitamins, filed January 2026.
- **Ondra** — Class 5 women's-health supplements, filed March 2026.
- **Statera** — Latin for scales, and already a US Class 5 supplement filing (March 2026), a UK Class 5 registration, and a wellness cliché on both sides of the Atlantic.
- **Pith** — both registers looked clean; a US company is selling brain-health supplements plus an app under the name today. Register searches alone would have passed it.
- **Fenna** — fenna.health taken in March, fennahealth.com taken **three days before the screen**.
- **Hale, Hearty, Limber, Brisk, Mettle, Lithe** — every English "condition" word already has a live Class 5, 9 or 44 mark in health.

Three rounds, 130-odd names, and the same lesson each time: anything that *means* health, *sounds* like wellness, or is a short pretty coinage has been claimed — often in the last twelve months. What survives is either obscure (Kelbrook, Thornby, Trutina), invented with hard consonants (Tovek, Mananu), or both.

## The decision

**Mananu.** Of the eight clean names it is the only one with no known conflict of any kind — no phonetic twin, no namesake company in an adjacent field, no famous-name owner, no circling registrations. After losing Kelvo to a supplement brand that appeared twelve days before the check, and watching Halden, Fenna and Lumae get occupied *during* this project, "nothing anywhere" is the property worth paying for. It also reads warmest for supplements and a health platform, and the `.com` is buyable.

**Trutina** is the better *word* — it literally means a pair of scales in Latin, the "tru-" carries the whole "weigh it, don't guess it" idea, and it sits in the Oura/Zoe pattern of a foreign word with a slant meaning. It loses on two facts: TRUTYNA is a pending US Class 5 supplement mark that sounds identical, and Trutina Therapeutics is a real biotech in Cambridge with the same spelling. Neither blocks a UK launch. Both are the kind of thing that costs money in year three. If Dani prefers meaning over cleanliness, Trutina is a defensible second choice — file UK first, and accept that US supplements would need a different name.

**Tovek** is the cleanest register of all and the coldest name. Right for the scale; wrong for a vitamin bottle.

**Kelbrook** remains the fallback.

# What you may claim, and what will get you in trouble

This is the positioning document and the ASA defence file in one. Read it before writing a single line of website copy, because the competitors in this category have written theirs badly and copying them is the fastest route to a complaint you cannot answer.

## The insight that keeps you honest and still wins

There are three sources of error in logging a meal:

- **(a) what is it** — identification
- **(b) how much is there** — quantity
- **(c) density, preparation, and fat absorbed in cooking**

A scale solves **(b) completely**. It does nothing for (a) or (c).

And the published evidence says **(c) is the dominant error**. A controlled-feeding study presented at NUTRITION 2026 (Hengist et al., NIH/NIDDK — 102 standardised meals weighed to 0.1 g in a metabolic kitchen, plus 200 more) found the leading apps underestimated meals by **250–345 kcal on average**, with **fat under-reported by roughly 30 g per meal**. Carbohydrate was the most consistent. Keto and high-fat meals were the worst.

So a founder who says "our scale solves accuracy" is making a claim that is both wrong and easy to disprove. The defensible product solves (b) with hardware and **attacks (c) with workflow** — which is exactly what the cooking-fat capture in the app does. Weigh the pan, weigh the oil, weigh what is left. No camera can see that.

## Claims you can make and substantiate

✅ **"Portion weight measured to ±0.1 g."** Metrological, about your own load cell, testable. Safe.

✅ **"Removes portion-size guesswork — the largest correctable source of error in photo-only logging."** Substantiated by the published range of image-based estimation error (Annals of Medicine 2023 systematic review of 52 studies: calorie estimation error 0.10% to 38.3%).

✅ **"Independent research found photo-only apps underestimate meals by 250–345 kcal on average, driven mainly by fat."** Cite it as a **NUTRITION 2026 conference abstract**, not as a peer-reviewed paper, because it is not one yet.

✅ **For bioimpedance, claim repeatability, not accuracy.** "Consistent, repeatable trend tracking under matched conditions." Repeatability you can test and prove in your own flat with a set of volunteers. Accuracy against a reference method you will lose.

✅ **"Weighs each ingredient separately, so a mixed plate is not one blended guess."** Simply describing what the product does.

## Claims that will cost you

❌ **Any single headline accuracy percentage for the system.** Cal AI published 90%, then quietly moved to 80%. Scanfit publishes 98.5%. Accuracy depends entirely on the meal — a bowl of weighed porridge and a restaurant curry are not the same problem — so a system-wide figure cannot be substantiated, and CAP requires documentary evidence for objective claims.

❌ **"DEXA-level accuracy" or any percentage agreement with DEXA for body fat.** The published literature contradicts it. A JMIR 2021 study of three consumer smart scales against DXA found weight accurate to about 0.3 kg but **fat-mass median absolute errors of −2.2 to −4.4 kg, with interquartile ranges reaching −8.0 kg**, concluding smart scales "are not accurate for body composition and should not replace" DXA.

❌ **Rate-or-amount weight loss claims.** CAP's guidance is explicit: claims referring to a rate or amount of weight loss are not permitted, and for overweight (non-obese) people you may not claim loss exceeding 2 lb per week. Cal AI's Play listing says "5+ pounds lost in first week." Do not copy that. Scanfit's "45% more fat loss in 90 days" has no methodology or control and would need rigorous trials on people.

❌ **Disease claims.** Framing visceral fat as cardiovascular risk, or "metabolic age" as health status, or anything about preventing lifestyle disease. That is the line that turns a wellness product into a medical device.

❌ **Before-and-after photos without signed, dated proof they are genuine.** Testimonials are never adequate substantiation on their own.

## What Scanfit is doing wrong, and why it matters to you

Their English site attacks "thin ITO-coated glass contact points" and claims their metal electrodes "conduct measurement signals 60–70× better." Their **Danish site**, for the same device, lists "Elektroder i ITO-belagt film" and a "6 mm hærdet glas med ITO-belægning" platform. Either the English copy is false or the two markets get different hardware under one name.

Their "independent third-party DEXA validation" is a PDF conducted at Shenzhen University General Hospital, n = 50, headed **"Supplier: Scanfit ApS"** — commissioned, not independent — with no DEXA device model, no correlation coefficients, no limits of agreement, and no derivation of the 98.5% figure. Their own per-metric numbers say fat mass 88%, which is not 98.5%.

And they market on "no subscription" while the App Store listing carries a **£/$599.99 per year** coaching tier.

Two implications. First, **do not build your marketing on the same foundations** — it is a complaint waiting to happen and you would be the smaller party. Second, when a UK journalist or a competitor eventually pulls that thread, you want to be the brand that publishes its methods.

## The thing to do that nobody in this category does

**Publish a methods page.** Your protocol, your sample size, your conditions, the full error distribution, the raw data. State which equations the app uses and their published standard errors — the app already shows them on every metric.

It costs a weekend, it is a genuine marketing asset in a category built on unverifiable percentages, and it *is* your substantiation file if the ASA ever asks.

## Copy that works

> **Weigh it. Don't guess it.**
>
> Photo calorie apps have to guess how much is on your plate. That guess is where the error lives — and the oil in the pan is invisible to a camera entirely.
>
> Mananu weighs every ingredient to a tenth of a gram, and uses the camera only for the part it is good at: working out what the food is.

Note what that does not say. No percentage. No comparison to a named competitor. No health outcome. Every sentence is a description of the mechanism, and every one is true.
